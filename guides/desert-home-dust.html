Keeping a desert home dust-free

In the desert you won’t get dust to zero, but you can make a big dent in how fast it comes back. Here’s what actually helps, and a simple cleaning rhythm that keeps up with it.

Why desert homes get dusty

Dry air, wind and open desert all around mean there’s always fine dust in the air, and summer storms can push a lot of it at once. It comes in on shoes and pets, through gaps around doors and windows, and through the air conditioning, which runs most of the year here. The fixes fall into two groups: let less in, and clean in a way that removes dust instead of moving it around.

Stop it at the door

Filters and vents

Your AC moves all the air in the house through its filter many times a day, so the filter does more dust control than anything else you own.

Dust so it stays gone

Feather dusters and dry rags mostly lift dust into the air, and it settles again an hour later. Trap it instead.

After a dust storm

When a big storm rolls through, wait until it has fully passed before cleaning, or you’ll do it twice.

A cleaning rhythm

Little and often beats a big clean once a month, because dust builds on itself. A rhythm that works for most homes here:

Weekly

  • Damp-dust surfaces you see every day
  • Vacuum, then mop hard floors
  • Shake out entry mats
  • Wipe bathroom and kitchen surfaces

Monthly

  • Ceiling fans and light fixtures
  • Blinds and window sills
  • Baseboards and door frames
  • Check the AC filter

Seasonally

  • Window and slider tracks
  • Vents and return grilles
  • Behind and under furniture
  • Door sweeps and weatherstripping

Fewer places for dust

Every open shelf, knickknack and fabric surface is somewhere for dust to land. You don’t have to live in an empty house, but grouping decor on fewer surfaces, using closed storage for the things you rarely touch, and washing throws and cushion covers now and then all mean less to wipe each week.
